# Part 3: Technology

## Discussion Questions & Sample Answers

---

### Q1: How has technology changed daily life?

**Sample Answer:**
> Technology has transformed virtually every aspect of daily life, often in ways we barely notice anymore because they've become so normal.
>
> Communication is perhaps the most obvious change. We can instantly connect with anyone worldwide through messaging, video calls, and social media. This has fundamentally changed how we maintain relationships, work, and get information.
>
> Work has been transformed. Many people, myself included, do jobs that wouldn't exist without technology. Even traditional jobs have been reshaped by digital tools.
>
> Access to information has democratized. Any question can be answered within seconds. Learning, research, and problem-solving that once required libraries and experts are now possible for anyone with a smartphone.
>
> Entertainment has expanded enormously. We have unlimited music, movies, games, and content available on demand, changing how we spend leisure time.
>
> Even mundane tasks—banking, shopping, navigation, home management—are now mediated by technology.
>
> While mostly positive, these changes also bring challenges: information overload, screen addiction, privacy concerns, and social isolation despite connection. The key is using technology intentionally rather than being used by it.

---

### Q2: What are the negative effects of technology on society?

**Sample Answer:**
> While technology brings many benefits, we should acknowledge significant negative effects.
>
> First, mental health impacts. Screen addiction, social media comparison, cyberbullying, and reduced face-to-face interaction contribute to increased anxiety and depression, especially among young people.
>
> Second, privacy erosion. We share enormous amounts of personal data, often without fully understanding how it's used. Surveillance capabilities have expanded dramatically.
>
> Third, economic disruption. Automation eliminates jobs, often faster than new ones are created. This contributes to inequality and economic insecurity for some populations.
>
> Fourth, misinformation spreads easily. Social media algorithms can amplify false information and create polarized echo chambers, undermining shared reality and civil discourse.
>
> Fifth, environmental impact. Electronics production and energy consumption for digital services have significant carbon footprints. E-waste is a growing problem.
>
> Sixth, reduced attention spans. Constant stimulation and information may be making sustained focus more difficult for many people.
>
> I'm not anti-technology—these problems don't negate technology's benefits—but we need to address them through thoughtful policy, design choices, and individual responsibility.

---

### Q3: Do you think artificial intelligence will replace human workers?

**Sample Answer:**
> AI will certainly replace some jobs, but I believe it will more commonly transform jobs rather than eliminate them entirely. This is relevant to my work as many wonder about AI in recruitment.
>
> Jobs most vulnerable to AI automation are those with routine, predictable tasks—data entry, basic analysis, some customer service, and manufacturing roles. These are already being affected.
>
> However, jobs requiring creativity, complex judgment, emotional intelligence, physical adaptability in unpredictable environments, and interpersonal connection are harder to automate and will likely remain human domains.
>
> More importantly, AI creates new jobs even as it eliminates others. The tech industry constantly generates new roles. The challenge is whether displaced workers can transition to new opportunities quickly enough.
>
> I think the future will involve humans and AI working together. AI handles routine tasks, while humans provide oversight, creativity, and interpersonal elements. This already happens in recruiting—AI can screen resumes, but human judgment remains essential for final decisions.
>
> Society needs to prepare through education, retraining programs, and potentially new economic models for a world where some traditional jobs no longer exist.

---

### Q4: Should there be limits on how technology is used?

**Sample Answer:**
> Yes, I believe reasonable limits on technology use are both necessary and appropriate, though implementation is challenging.
>
> Certain uses should clearly be restricted: technology for mass surveillance, autonomous weapons, or invasions of privacy without consent are examples where limits serve important values.
>
> Children's exposure to technology probably needs limits. Screen time restrictions, age requirements for social media, and design guidelines that don't exploit psychological vulnerabilities seem reasonable.
>
> Data collection and use should be regulated. People should know what data is collected about them and have meaningful choices about its use.
>
> However, drawing limits is complicated. Technology develops faster than regulations. Overly restrictive limits could stifle innovation and place countries at competitive disadvantages. And defining what's harmful isn't always clear-cut.
>
> I think the approach should be: establish clear principles about what technology shouldn't do (harm privacy, exploit children, spread misinformation), then develop flexible regulations that adapt as technology evolves. This requires ongoing dialogue between technologists, policymakers, and society.

---

### Q5: How do you think technology will develop in the future?

**Sample Answer:**
> Predicting technology is notoriously difficult, but several trends seem likely to continue.
>
> Artificial intelligence will become more capable and embedded in more aspects of life. We'll interact with AI assistants more naturally, and AI will handle increasingly complex tasks.
>
> Connectivity will expand and speed up. 5G and beyond will enable new applications, and more devices will be connected. The Internet of Things will become more pervasive.
>
> Virtual and augmented reality may finally achieve mainstream adoption, changing how we work, learn, and entertain ourselves.
>
> Healthcare technology will advance significantly—personalized medicine, remote monitoring, AI diagnosis, and perhaps extending healthy lifespan.
>
> Clean energy technology will hopefully progress, driven by climate change urgency. Electric vehicles, improved batteries, and renewable energy seem set to expand.
>
> However, development depends on investment, policy, and unpredictable breakthroughs. I'm optimistic about technology's potential but concerned about ensuring benefits are widely shared and risks are managed.
>
> The key question isn't just what technology we develop, but how wisely we deploy it and whether we address its downsides as we embrace its benefits.
