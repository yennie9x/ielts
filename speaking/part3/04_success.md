# Part 3: Success

## Discussion Questions & Sample Answers

---

### Q1: How do you define success?

**Sample Answer:**
> I believe success is achieving meaningful goals while maintaining your values and well-being. It's not just about money or status, though those can be components. True success, in my view, involves fulfillment in multiple areas—career, relationships, health, and personal growth.
>
> For some people, success might mean building a thriving business. For others, it could be raising happy children or making a difference in their community. The definition is personal.
>
> What I think is crucial is that success should be self-defined, not imposed by society. If someone achieves everything society says is "successful" but feels empty inside, I wouldn't call that true success. Conversely, someone living simply but with purpose and contentment has achieved something valuable.
>
> For me personally, success means doing work I find meaningful, maintaining good relationships, and continuously growing as a person.

---

### Q2: Is success more about luck or hard work?

**Sample Answer:**
> I think it's a combination of both, but hard work generally plays a larger role.
>
> Hard work is essential because without effort, talent and opportunity go wasted. Even people who seem lucky usually worked hard to be ready when opportunities came. As a recruiter, I've seen that the candidates who consistently improve their skills and prepare thoroughly tend to succeed more often than those who don't, regardless of initial circumstances.
>
> However, I can't ignore the role of luck—being born into certain circumstances, meeting the right people, being in the right place at the right time. I was lucky to have supportive parents who valued education. Someone equally hardworking in different circumstances might not have the same opportunities.
>
> The practical takeaway is that we can't control luck, but we can control effort. So focusing on hard work while remaining grateful for fortunate circumstances seems like the wisest approach.

---

### Q3: Do you think the definition of success has changed over generations?

**Sample Answer:**
> Yes, I believe the definition of success has evolved considerably across generations.
>
> For my grandparents' generation, success was largely about survival and stability—having enough food, a roof over your head, and providing for your family. For my parents, it expanded to include education, stable employment, and perhaps owning property.
>
> For my generation and younger, success often includes additional dimensions: work-life balance, personal fulfillment, making an impact, flexibility, and experiences rather than just material possessions. Many young people prioritize meaningful work over high salaries, or value time freedom over climbing corporate ladders.
>
> This shift is partly because basic needs are more easily met now, so people can focus on higher-order goals. It's also influenced by social media, which exposes us to diverse lifestyles, and by changing values around mental health and well-being.
>
> I think this evolution is generally positive—a broader definition of success allows more people to feel they can achieve it.

---

### Q4: Does success always bring happiness?

**Sample Answer:**
> Not necessarily. There are many examples of successful people—by conventional measures—who are deeply unhappy. Wealthy business people, famous celebrities, accomplished professionals can still suffer from loneliness, stress, or lack of purpose.
>
> Success might bring temporary happiness when you achieve a goal, but lasting happiness depends on other factors: relationships, health, purpose, gratitude, and alignment between your actions and values.
>
> Sometimes the pursuit of success actually undermines happiness—people sacrifice relationships, health, and present enjoyment for future success that may not bring the fulfillment they expected.
>
> However, success can contribute to happiness when it's defined meaningfully. If your definition of success includes relationships and well-being, then achieving it probably does increase happiness. The problem is when success is narrowly defined as wealth or status alone.
>
> I think the relationship between success and happiness depends entirely on how you define success and what you're willing to sacrifice for it.

---

### Q5: What role does failure play in achieving success?

**Sample Answer:**
> Failure is often essential to success. Almost every successful person has failed multiple times along the way. Failure teaches lessons that success cannot—it shows you what doesn't work, reveals weaknesses to address, and builds resilience.
>
> When I was job hunting after graduation, I faced many rejections before landing my first recruitment position. Each rejection taught me something—about interview skills, about what employers wanted, about my own gaps. Without those failures, I wouldn't have improved.
>
> Failure also tests your commitment. If you give up after one failure, you probably weren't going to succeed anyway. Persistence through failure separates those who eventually succeed from those who don't.
>
> However, failure is only valuable if you learn from it. Failing repeatedly without reflecting and adjusting is just repetition, not growth.
>
> Culturally, I think we need to become more accepting of failure. In Vietnam, there's sometimes too much stigma around failing, which can discourage people from taking necessary risks. Embracing failure as part of the journey to success would benefit everyone.
