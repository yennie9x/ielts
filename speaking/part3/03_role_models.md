# Part 3: Role Models & Influence

## Discussion Questions & Sample Answers

---

### Q1: Why do people need role models?

**Sample Answer:**
> People need role models because they provide guidance, inspiration, and a vision of what's possible. When we see someone who has achieved something we aspire to, it helps us believe that we can do it too.
>
> Role models also serve as learning shortcuts. Instead of figuring everything out ourselves, we can observe how successful people behave, what decisions they make, and how they handle challenges. This is especially valuable for young people who are still forming their identities and values.
>
> Additionally, role models can motivate us during difficult times. When I face challenges at work, thinking about people who overcame greater obstacles helps me persist. For example, knowing that successful tech leaders often started from humble backgrounds, like my colleague who came from a rural area and became a head of engineering, gives me hope and motivation.

---

### Q2: Who has more influence on young people—parents or celebrities?

**Sample Answer:**
> I believe parents generally have deeper, more lasting influence, but celebrities can have significant impact on surface-level preferences and behaviors.
>
> Parents shape fundamental values, beliefs, and character traits through daily interaction over many years. The influence of family is constant and personal. When I think about my own values—hard work, honesty, the importance of education—these came primarily from my parents in Chau Doc, not from any celebrity.
>
> However, celebrities can influence young people's fashion choices, interests, language, and aspirations. With social media, celebrities seem more accessible than ever, and young people might spend hours consuming their content.
>
> The concern is when celebrity influence contradicts parental values or promotes harmful behaviors. I think the ideal is for parents to remain engaged with their children and discuss the messages coming from media, helping them think critically about what they see.

---

### Q3: Are the role models today different from those in the past?

**Sample Answer:**
> Yes, role models have changed significantly, partly due to changes in media and society.
>
> In the past, role models were often people in one's immediate community—teachers, religious leaders, local business people—or historical figures and national heroes. Access to celebrities was limited.
>
> Today, social media has democratized fame. Role models now include influencers, YouTubers, gamers, and entrepreneurs who built businesses from their bedrooms. Young people might look up to someone who has millions of followers but no traditional credentials.
>
> There are pros and cons to this shift. On one hand, today's role models often seem more relatable and accessible. On the other hand, some influencers promote unrealistic lifestyles or questionable values. The variety of role models available today means young people need to be more discerning about who they admire and why.

---

### Q4: Should famous people be expected to be good role models?

**Sample Answer:**
> This is a complex issue. On one hand, I understand the argument that famous people didn't necessarily sign up to be role models—they might be talented athletes, musicians, or actors, but that doesn't mean they're qualified to be moral guides.
>
> On the other hand, with fame comes influence, and influential people do bear some responsibility for how they use that platform. When millions of young people are watching, promoting harmful behaviors is irresponsible.
>
> I think the compromise is this: we shouldn't expect celebrities to be perfect, but we can expect them to avoid actively promoting harmful things. It's also important for parents and educators to teach young people that celebrities are human, flawed, and not suitable models for everything in life.
>
> Ultimately, the responsibility lies with individuals to choose their role models wisely, not to blindly follow anyone just because they're famous.

---

### Q5: How can teachers be positive role models for students?

**Sample Answer:**
> Teachers can be powerful role models in several ways. First, through their actions—demonstrating qualities like patience, fairness, dedication, and lifelong learning. Students watch how teachers handle difficult situations, treat students, and approach their work.
>
> Second, teachers influence through their attitude toward learning. A teacher who shows genuine enthusiasm for their subject can inspire students to develop similar passion. I had an English teacher who clearly loved the language, and that enthusiasm was contagious.
>
> Third, teachers can share their own stories and challenges, showing students that everyone faces obstacles. This makes success seem more achievable.
>
> However, teachers aren't just academic role models—they also model professional behavior, communication, and interpersonal skills. Students learn not just subject matter but how to behave in the world by observing teachers.
>
> Of course, teachers are human and shouldn't be expected to be perfect. But being aware of their influence and striving to model positive behaviors can have a lasting impact on students' lives.
