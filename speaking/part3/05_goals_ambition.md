# Part 3: Goals & Ambition

## Discussion Questions & Sample Answers

---

### Q1: Why is it important to have goals in life?

**Sample Answer:**
> Goals provide direction and purpose. Without goals, it's easy to drift through life without making meaningful progress. Goals give you something to work toward, which creates motivation and a sense of accomplishment when achieved.
>
> From a practical perspective, goals help with decision-making. When you have clear objectives, it's easier to decide how to spend your time and resources. For example, my goal of building a career in Ho Chi Minh City helped me decide to move from Chau Doc—a difficult decision that became clearer when I had a goal in mind.
>
> Goals also contribute to well-being. Research shows that people with goals, even simple ones, tend to be happier and more satisfied with life than those without direction. The process of working toward something meaningful is itself fulfilling.
>
> However, I'd add that it's important to be flexible about goals. Life changes, and sometimes goals need to change too. Rigid attachment to outdated goals can be counterproductive.

---

### Q2: Do you think young people today are more or less ambitious than previous generations?

**Sample Answer:**
> I think young people today are equally ambitious, but their ambitions often look different from previous generations, which can be misinterpreted as lack of ambition.
>
> Older generations might have aspired to traditional markers of success: stable jobs, home ownership, climbing the career ladder in one company. Young people today might instead prioritize starting their own businesses, achieving work-life balance, traveling, or making a social impact. These are ambitious goals, just different ones.
>
> Some might argue that young people are less willing to work extremely long hours or sacrifice everything for career advancement. But I'd say this reflects different priorities rather than less ambition—perhaps a healthier recognition that success shouldn't come at the cost of everything else.
>
> There's also the reality that economic conditions have changed. Property is more expensive, job security is less guaranteed, and traditional paths to success are less clear. Young people are adapting their ambitions to new realities, which requires its own form of resourcefulness.

---

### Q3: Is it better to set realistic goals or ambitious goals?

**Sample Answer:**
> I think the ideal is a combination—having an ambitious long-term vision while setting realistic short-term goals along the way.
>
> Purely realistic goals might limit your potential. If you only aim for what seems easily achievable, you might never discover what you're truly capable of. Some degree of ambition pushes you to grow and try harder than you otherwise would.
>
> However, if goals are too ambitious and disconnected from reality, they can lead to frustration and demotivation when you consistently fail to reach them. This is why breaking ambitious goals into smaller, achievable steps is important.
>
> For example, buying an apartment in Ho Chi Minh City is an ambitious goal for me. But I approach it through realistic steps: saving a certain amount monthly, learning about the market, potentially increasing my income. The big goal motivates me; the small goals keep me grounded.
>
> I think the key is finding that balance where goals stretch you but remain achievable with effort.

---

### Q4: How can people stay motivated to achieve their goals?

**Sample Answer:**
> Staying motivated is challenging, especially for long-term goals. Several strategies can help.
>
> First, breaking large goals into smaller milestones creates regular feelings of progress and achievement. Celebrating small wins maintains momentum.
>
> Second, connecting goals to deeper values and purposes helps. When you understand why a goal matters to you, motivation comes more naturally than when pursuing something abstract.
>
> Third, creating accountability helps. Telling others about your goals, finding partners with similar objectives, or tracking progress publicly can provide external motivation when internal motivation wavers.
>
> Fourth, maintaining good habits and routines reduces reliance on motivation alone. When positive actions become automatic, you don't need to be motivated every day to make progress.
>
> Finally, being kind to yourself during setbacks is important. Motivation naturally fluctuates, and treating temporary failures as learning opportunities rather than reasons to quit helps maintain long-term motivation.

---

### Q5: Do you think children should be encouraged to have goals from an early age?

**Sample Answer:**
> Yes, but with appropriate guidance and without excessive pressure.
>
> Teaching children about goals helps them develop important life skills: planning, delayed gratification, persistence, and time management. These skills serve them throughout life regardless of what specific goals they pursue.
>
> However, children should be allowed to set their own goals rather than having parents' ambitions imposed on them. Children who only pursue goals their parents set may lose intrinsic motivation or struggle with identity later.
>
> The goals should also be age-appropriate and flexible. A young child's goal might be reading a book or learning to ride a bicycle—not career planning. And children should learn that it's okay to change goals as they grow and discover new interests.
>
> There's also risk in overemphasizing goals. Children also need unstructured time for play and exploration. If every moment is goal-directed, they might miss opportunities for creativity and self-discovery.
>
> So yes, encourage goals, but balance it with freedom and ensure goals remain the child's own rather than imposed expectations.
