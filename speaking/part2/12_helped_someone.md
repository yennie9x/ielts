# Part 2: Describe a time you helped someone

## Cue Card

**Describe a time when you helped someone.**

You should say:
- Who you helped
- What the situation was
- How you helped them
- And explain how you felt about helping them

---

## Sample Answer

I'd like to talk about a time when I helped a job candidate who was struggling with his career transition. It happened about a year ago and remains one of my most rewarding experiences as a recruiter.

The person I helped was a young developer named Huy who wanted to switch from a small local company to a larger tech firm. He had good technical skills but lacked confidence in interviews and didn't know how to present himself professionally. He had already failed several interviews before reaching out to me through a mutual connection.

The situation was that Huy felt stuck and was considering giving up on his dream of working at a top tech company. When I spoke with him, I recognized potential that he couldn't see in himself. His problem wasn't ability—it was presentation and interview preparation.

I helped him in several ways. First, I reviewed his resume and helped him restructure it to highlight his achievements rather than just listing responsibilities. Then, I conducted mock interviews with him, giving feedback on his answers, body language, and communication style. I also coached him on how to research companies and ask intelligent questions during interviews. We spent several weeks preparing before I referred him to a position at one of my client companies.

The result was that Huy got the job. When he called to tell me the news, he was almost in tears—he said this opportunity would change his life. That moment made me feel incredibly fulfilled. It reminded me why I chose this career. Beyond just filling positions, recruitment allows me to genuinely impact people's lives.

What made this experience special was that I could relate to Huy's journey. I remember my own struggles when I first moved to Ho Chi Minh City from Chau Doc. Someone helped me then, and being able to pay it forward felt deeply meaningful.
