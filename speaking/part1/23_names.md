# IELTS Speaking Part 1: Names

## Questions

1. Does your name have any special meaning?
2. Do you like your name?
3. Would you like to change your name?
4. Who gave you your name?
5. Are names important in your culture?
6. How do people choose names for children in your country?
7. Do you have a nickname?
8. Is it easy for foreigners to pronounce your name?

---

## Sample Answers

### Q: Does your name have any special meaning?
> Yes, like most Vietnamese names, mine has a meaning. My parents chose it hoping I would have certain positive qualities. Vietnamese names often come from words that represent virtues, nature, or good wishes.

### Q: Do you like your name?
> Yes, I do. I've grown attached to it over the years. It's part of my identity, and I think it suits me well. I'm grateful my parents chose a name that isn't too difficult to pronounce.

### Q: Would you like to change your name?
> No, I'm happy with my name. Some people adopt English names for work, especially when dealing with international clients, but I prefer using my Vietnamese name. It reflects my heritage and who I am.

### Q: Who gave you your name?
> My parents chose my name, though I believe my grandparents also had some input. In Vietnamese families, naming a child is often a family decision, and sometimes elders are consulted for their wisdom.

### Q: Are names important in your culture?
> Very important. Vietnamese people believe names can influence a person's destiny. Parents carefully choose names with good meanings and pleasant sounds. We also have traditions about avoiding certain names that sound similar to words with bad meanings.

### Q: How do people choose names for children in your country?
> Parents typically choose names with positive meanings—words representing success, beauty, strength, or happiness. Some families consult fortune tellers or use the child's birth date and time to select an auspicious name. The family name comes first, followed by a middle name and given name.

### Q: Do you have a nickname?
> Yes, close friends and family use a shortened version of my name or a playful nickname. In Vietnam, it's common to have different names or nicknames used by different groups—formal name for work, nicknames for friends.

### Q: Is it easy for foreigners to pronounce your name?
> It can be challenging because Vietnamese is a tonal language. The tones and certain sounds don't exist in English, so foreigners often approximate it. I don't mind though—I appreciate when people make an effort to say it correctly.

## Useful Vocabulary

- **Virtues** - đức tính tốt
- **Heritage** - di sản, nguồn gốc
- **Consulted** - được hỏi ý kiến
- **Destiny** - số phận
- **Fortune teller** - thầy bói
- **Auspicious** - may mắn, tốt lành
- **Tonal language** - ngôn ngữ có thanh điệu
- **Approximate** - phát âm gần đúng
