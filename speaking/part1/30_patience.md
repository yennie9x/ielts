# IELTS Speaking Part 1: Patience

## Questions

1. Are you a patient person?
2. When do you need to be patient?
3. Have you become more patient as you've gotten older?
4. What makes you lose patience?
5. Do you think patience is important?
6. How do you stay patient in difficult situations?
7. Are people in your country generally patient?
8. Can patience be learned?

---

## Sample Answers

### Q: Are you a patient person?
> I'd say I'm moderately patient. In my job as a recruiter, patience is essential—the hiring process can be lengthy, and I need to wait for candidates and clients. But I'll admit, in everyday situations like traffic jams, my patience can be tested.

### Q: When do you need to be patient?
> At work, definitely. Recruiting involves a lot of waiting—for candidates to respond, for interview feedback, for final decisions. I also need patience when dealing with difficult situations or when things don't go as planned.

### Q: Have you become more patient as you've gotten older?
> Yes, I think so. When I was younger, I wanted everything immediately. Now I understand that good things take time—career growth, relationships, personal goals. Experience has taught me that rushing rarely leads to better outcomes.

### Q: What makes you lose patience?
> Traffic in Ho Chi Minh City is a big one—sitting in a jam when you're already running late is frustrating. I also get impatient when technology doesn't work properly, like slow internet or apps crashing. And dealing with unreasonable people can test my patience.

### Q: Do you think patience is important?
> Absolutely. Patience helps you make better decisions because you're not rushing. It reduces stress and helps maintain good relationships. In my work, patience is crucial for building rapport with candidates and closing successful placements.

### Q: How do you stay patient in difficult situations?
> I try to take deep breaths and remind myself that getting frustrated won't help. I focus on what I can control rather than what I can't. Sometimes listening to music or stepping away for a moment helps me reset.

### Q: Are people in your country generally patient?
> It's mixed. Vietnamese people can be very patient in certain situations—like waiting in queues or dealing with bureaucracy. But in traffic, patience tends to run thin. I think the fast pace of city life has made people less patient overall.

### Q: Can patience be learned?
> Yes, I believe so. It's like a muscle that you can strengthen with practice. Mindfulness and meditation can help, as can consciously choosing to respond calmly instead of reacting immediately. It takes effort, but patience can definitely be developed.

## Useful Vocabulary

- **Moderately** - vừa phải
- **Essential** - cần thiết
- **Outcomes** - kết quả
- **Frustrating** - bực bội
- **Building rapport** - xây dựng mối quan hệ
- **Bureaucracy** - thủ tục hành chính
- **Mindfulness** - chánh niệm
- **Consciously** - một cách có ý thức
