## Speaking 1 — A time you could not keep your promise

**Type:** Speaking · Part 2  
**Shape:** I try to keep promises → I promised to go → what went wrong → I said sorry

Well, **keeping a promise** is really important to me. I want to be a **reliable** person, so people can **trust me** and **count on me**. But sometimes I still can’t **keep my word**.

A few months ago, a close friend invited me to her birthday party at 7 p.m. We’ve **known each other since high school**, so there was **no way I could miss it**.

That day, I hit bad traffic on the way home because of the rain. It usually takes 20 minutes, but that day it took almost an hour.

I felt exhausted, so I **took a nap**. When I woke up, it was almost midnight, and I had ten **missed calls** from her. I **messaged her right away** to **apologize**.

I also gave her a small present **the following day**. Luckily, she understood and **forgave me**.

Anyway, it was a pity, and I never want it to happen again.

**Collocation:** break a promise · keep my word · count on me · get stuck in a traffic jam · take a nap · apologize for · the following day · forgive someone

**Fixes:** keep my words → keep my word · I want to bed → I went to bed · it take → it takes · midnihgt → midnight

## Speaking 2 — A reliable person

**Type:** Speaking  
**Shape:** who → what she does → why she is my role model

If I have to name someone I really **trust**, it’s my mum.

She doesn’t **make promises easily**, but once she does, she **keeps her word** and **does her best** to follow through. I can **count on her** in any situation.

She’s a real **role model** for me. She always says **trust is earned** — you have to earn it from the people around you.

**Collocation:** make promises easily · keep her word · follow through · count on her · role model · trust is earned

## Writing — How to build confidence?

**Type:** problem–solution  
**Shape:** intro → believe you have strengths → work + support → end

These days, many people **lack confidence** at school and at work. If this lasts for a long time, it can **hold them back**. The good news is that there are simple things we can do.

The first step is to **believe we have some strengths**. Everyone is good at something, even if they do not see it yet. For example, a shy student may write well or help friends — that is already a real strength.

More importantly, confidence **grows when we work**. If we practise a skill and remember things we have already done well, we feel stronger **when life gets hard**. Teachers, parents, and friends can also help by **encouraging** us.

In the end, confidence is not just a feeling. If we work hard and remember our strengths, we are **more likely to** do well at school and in life.

**Collocation**
- lack confidence
- hold someone back
- believe we have some strengths
- when life gets hard
- more likely to

**Grammar**
- If this lasts ..., it can ...
- If we ..., we ...
- when life gets hard
